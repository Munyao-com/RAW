About

Self-taught in East Oakland. Shown at the de Young Museum.

Roosevelt Washington grew up in East Oakland, California. Nobody handed him a path into art. A friendly rivalry and years of practice made him the artist he is today.

The story

Roosevelt found his medium by accident. One day he forgot his pastels and had only pastel pencils with him. He drew anyway. The fine point let him build skin, light and feeling layer by layer, and he never went back.

He later moved to Virginia to be near family. Working overtime, he saved enough to build a studio of his own. Everything that leaves it is done by his hand. A single piece can take weeks: hundreds of fine strokes to get the weight of a sleeping child, the warmth of skin, the way light rests on a hand.

He keeps his distance from art trends on purpose. Each piece is his own, so it stays original and holds its value for the people who collect it.

Black fathers. Black mothers. Sisters and brothers. Love you can see.

Roosevelt's best-known works show Black men holding babies. He draws them because the world rarely shows that picture, and because he knows it is true. A woman once asked him why he chose that subject. When she saw the finished piece, she called it beautiful.
